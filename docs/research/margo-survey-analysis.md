# Margo survey analysis — August 2026

**Source:** Google Form export *How We Use Music (5–7 Minute Survey)*, 56 consented responses, 19–20 August 2026.  
**Read against:** the live 11-slide deck (`Deck_for_Margo.html` / `Margo_Pitch.pdf`), `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `PROJECT_MAP.md`, `MARGO_BRAND.md`, and the actual product loop (Lyric Feed, snippet playback, Lyric Back as a lyric reply, lyric cards, hosted independents, Philippines first).  
**This file is research only.** It does not change the product or the deck.

---

## Headline finding

The survey **validates the habit Margo is built around**. It does **not** yet validate the product loop the company needs in the next 12 months.

Almost everyone in this sample already sends, receives, or posts music to say something. Lyrics and “a particular line” carry meaning as often as “the whole song.” Specific parts of songs and lyric lines are how they share today. That is the deck’s opening claim, and the data supports it.

When the same people are asked what a *platform like this* should be, they lean toward **mood, recommendations, new music, and full songs**. “Responding through music” (the closest proxy for Lyric Back) is a minority top-pick. “Notifications and responses” is a weak reason to come back. Stated daily use is high and should not be treated as a retention forecast.

So: **the premise is real. The risk is that people describe Margo’s problem, then ask for a nicer Spotify.** The $10k year should still prove whether the lyric-social loop holds, not whether people like music.

**Sample caveats (read every number through these):**

- N = 56. All said yes to participate. No one selected “not interesting.”
- This is almost certainly a **Philippines-leaning convenience sample** (Trinity University of Asia emails, Filipino names, timing). That is useful for a PH-first launch. It is not a global or MENA sample. The form did not ask city or country.
- Q6 (“select all that apply” on reasons for sending a song) was broken. At least one respondent wrote that only one option could be selected. Do not treat Q6 as multi-select evidence.
- Q13–Q16 are **hypothetical intent** about “a platform like this.” The export does not include the concept copy they saw. Interest is not a Margo signup.
- Q14 said “up to 5” parts; several people picked more. Rank order is still usable; treat counts as mentions, not a clean forced choice.
- One near-duplicate pair (same timestamp 8/20 4:17:49, answers identical except pay intent). Leaving both in does not change the headline.

## 01 — Margo: “Talk in lyrics.”

**Deck claim:** People already use songs to say what they feel. Margo is a social platform built around that habit.

**Supports**

- 50/56 (89%) listen every day or several times a day. This is a heavy-music sample, which is who Margo is for, not “everyone.”
- 31/56 (55%) already use music for “expressing how I feel,” behind only relaxing and mood.
- 23/56 said **lyrics** make a song meaningful. Another 5 said a particular line or part. Together, 28/56 (50%) put words/line first, ahead of melody (6) or “the whole song” as a meaning-carrier in Q7.
- When a song communicates something, 23/56 named **a particular lyric or line** and 16/56 named **music and words together**. Combined, 39/56 (70%) put language in the meaning. “The whole song” is 13/56 (23%).

**Contradicts / complicates**

- Music is still mostly **personal utility**: relaxing (46), mood (39), entertainment (32). Expression is real, not the majority daily job of music.
- “Connecting with others” as a use of music is only 12/56 (21%). The habit is more “I use a song to say something” than “I go online to talk with people through songs.”

**Silent**

- Nobody was asked whether they know Margo or have used trymargo.com.

**Interpretation:** The one-line brand claim is fair. Do not upgrade it to “people want a new social network.” They already talk *with* songs. They did not say they are missing a feed.

## 02 — The habit already exists (stories, chats, not a dedicated home)

**Deck claim:** Photos, thoughts, and video have homes. Lyrics still live in stories and chats.

**Supports**

- 53/56 (95%) have **sent** a song so someone would understand something (only 3 never). 46/56 (82%) do it sometimes or often.
- 51/56 (91%) have **received** a song as communication. 45/56 (80%) sometimes or often.
- Only 2/56 never sent *and* never received. The two-way habit is the default in this sample, not a niche.
- 52/56 (93%) have shared a song publicly (story, status, post) because it represented a feeling. 50/56 (89%) do that sometimes or often.
- Why they share: **expressing how I feel** 47/56 (84%). Indirect communication 22/56 (39%). Introducing an artist is only 10/56 (18%).
- How they share today: **specific part of a song** 29/56 (52%), **lyrics or lyric line** 27/56 (48%), full song 23/56 (41%), song with a photo/video 16/56 (29%), caption containing lyrics 11/56 (20%), screenshot only 5/56 (9%).

**Contradicts / complicates**

- The behavior is already satisfied on other apps. High public-sharing frequency is evidence the habit **leaks everywhere**, and also evidence it may not *hurt* enough to switch homes. The deck is honest that Instagram/TikTok/X already host this informally. The survey does not show those tools failing. It shows they are in use.
- “I like the song” is still the second-largest share reason (35/56). A lot of sharing is taste display, not a message.

**Silent**

- No question named WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok, or Facebook as the channel. We cannot rank which incumbent holds the habit in the Philippines.

**Interpretation:** Slide 02 is the strongest slide in the deck relative to this data. The competitive claim (“not a dedicated home”) is a product bet, not something respondents complained about unprompted.

## 03 — From lyric to social interaction (the loop / Lyric Back)

**Deck claim:** Someone writes. Someone resonates. They pass it on. They come back. Lyric Back = reply with a lyric, no comment box.

**Supports**

- Sending and receiving songs as messages is already a **reply-shaped** behavior in the wild (Q4–Q5). That is closer to Lyric Back than a comment box is.
- 17/56 (30%) picked **“Responding through music”** as a part that would interest them. 14/56 (25%) picked **music conversations**.
- Indirect communication is a real motive for sharing (22/56). That matches “say it with a line instead of a paragraph.”

**Contradicts / complicates**

- Among concept features, **expressing** (42/56, 75%) far beats **responding** (17/56, 30%). People want to *post a feeling*. Fewer name *answering with a lyric* as a top draw.
- What would make them come back: mood (36), meaningful moments (35), personalized recommendations (33), new music (31). **Notifications and responses: 8/56 (14%).** Friends using it: 16/56. That is a weak stated retention loop for Lyric Back specifically.
- Following other users: 4/56 (7%). The social graph they imagine is not “I follow people to talk.”

**Silent**

- The form never described “no comment box” or “reply only with a lyric.” We cannot say they prefer Lyric Back to comments. We can only say they already send songs instead of (or as) messages.

**Interpretation:** Treat Lyric Back as **mechanically aligned with existing 1:1 behavior**, not as a proven feed mechanic. The 12-month question “do people come back?” is still the right test, because this survey’s “come back” answers point at discovery and mood, not replies.

## 04 — What we built

### Lyric Feed (discover lyric moments from other people)

**Supports:** 19/56 (34%) want discovering music through other people’s posts. 31/56 (55%) want discovery by emotion or situation. 27/56 (48%) want sharing meaningful moments from songs.

**Complicates:** 38/56 (68%) picked **discovering new music** and 34/56 (61%) picked **listening to full songs**. The feed they are picturing can collapse into a catalog/recommendation product unless Margo keeps the unit of content as a *line and a feeling*.

### Hear the moment (snippet: tap a line, hear that part)

**Supports:** Sharing “a specific part of a song” is the single most common share format (29/56). Q7’s top meaning-carrier is a particular lyric or line (23/56). Memory attachment is almost universal (48/56 sometimes/often).

**Complicates:** One open-ended answer pushed *against* clip culture: people posting only the viral last line of a song (Rex Orange County example) instead of the whole work. Snippets match current behavior and will annoy people who already resent it.

**Silent:** No one was asked whether they want instant audio on a tapped line. Format evidence is about *sharing* a part, not *hearing* it in-app.

### Lyric Back (reply with a lyric)

Covered in section 03. Support is behavioral (they already send songs). Stated product interest is secondary to expression, full songs, and discovery.

### Playlists and karaoke / full-song listening

**Supports, and it is a pull away from the wedge:** “Listening to full songs” is the third-most-selected part of the concept (34/56). Premium interest includes higher-quality listening (31/56) and exclusive artist/music content (22/56). Open-ended answers ask for songs that are worth hearing whole.

**Complicates:** Hosted full-song listening is live only for independents Margo hosts. Majors redirect to Spotify. If this sample’s #2/#3 desires are “new music” and “full songs,” the live product will feel incomplete unless the *lyric moment* is clearly better than opening Spotify.

### Lyric cards (visual post you share outside Margo)

**Supports**

- 47/56 (84%) said a designed card / GIF-style / styled text would be very or somewhat useful (31 very, 16 somewhat). Nobody said useless.
- 52/56 (93%) would likely or very likely share a perfect music moment outside the platform (29 very likely).
- They already make visual shares: photo/video with a song (16), lyric captions (11), stickers (5), screenshots (5).

**Complicates**

- Current screenshot use is low (9%). Cards are a **stated upgrade** to a behavior that often already uses the full song, a lyric paste, or a video, not a poster.
- Only 12/56 (21%) put “sharing music moments outside the platform” in their top parts of the concept. They will share out if the moment is perfect. They did not rank export as a reason to *join*.

**Interpretation:** Cards are a plausible growth mechanic (export → IG/WhatsApp → back). They are not the reason people said they would open the app.

### Independent artists (upload, we host, they keep the rights)

**Supports:** Several open-ended answers ask for underrated / smaller / independent artists instead of viral charts. Person-to-person discovery is already how they find songs (49/56 sometimes/often discovered a song because someone shared it).

**Complicates:** “Following artists” as a concept feature is only 11/56 (20%). Artist *presence* raises likelihood (next section). Artist *following* is not what they picked as the product.

**Silent:** No question on uploading original music, keeping rights, or being an artist. This is a listener survey.

## 05 — Why Margo can become a company (flywheel, premium later, ads later)

**Deck flywheel:** People → lyrics → music → artists → more people. Immediate goal is not monetization. Prove they come back.

**Supports**

- Person-shared discovery is already the music graph (Q12).
- If artists they like were active: 48/56 (86%) more or much more likely (29 much more). That is the “artists make the room worth entering” half of the flywheel.
- If friends were using it: also 48/56 (86%) more or much more likely (24 much more).

**Complicates**

- Stated *return* drivers are mood, recommendations, and new music, not “more artists I discovered through lyrics.” The flywheel is a theory of how *Margo* creates discovery. Respondents described how *Spotify-like recs* create it.
- Ads: 33/56 (59%) comfortable or very comfortable with a free, ad-supported product. Only 4/56 uncomfortable. Neutral is 19/56. The deck treats advertising as a later, careful move. This sample is not anti-ad. That does **not** mean Margo should turn ads on this year. It means “ads later” is not blocked by hostility in this group.
- Premium: 32/56 (57%) would probably or definitely consider paying. 17/56 not sure. 7/56 probably/definitely not. The price they call reasonable:

  | Monthly price | n | % of 56 |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | $1–$2 | 24 | 43% |
  | Less than $1 | 13 | 23% |
  | Would not pay | 10 | 18% |
  | $3–$5 | 7 | 13% |
  | $6–$10 | 2 | 4% |

  Among the 9 who said **definitely** pay, 5 picked $3–$5 and 3 picked $1–$2. Soft intent (“probably”) piles up at $1–$2 (13 of 23).

- What they would pay *for*: **no advertisements** 48/56 (86%), better personalization 32, higher-quality listening 31, advanced discovery 26. Advanced lyric features 21. More ways to create music moments 19. Advanced social features 18.

**Interpretation:** $1–$2/month is the **mode**, not a proven willingness to pay. It is realistic as a *later* price *if* premium is “no ads + better listening/personalization.” It is **not** a reason to sell premium in year one. It also pulls premium toward DSP features (ad-free, quality, recs), not toward Lyric Back. The deck’s line “the immediate goal is not monetization” is the correct reading of this table.

## 06–07 — Philippines first, then SEA, MENA as a second door

**Supports**

- The sample looks like the first market: TUA (Quezon City) emails, Filipino names, English-language form completed quickly. For a PH-first launch, these are the right people to have asked.
- Music is daily, mobile-share formats are already in use (stories/status/post, stickers, captions). That matches “start where the behavior is already strong.”

**Contradicts / complicates**

- There is **no geography field**. We cannot report “Filipino users want X” as a national finding. We can say “this August 2026 form, likely circulated in a PH student/young-adult network.”
- **Silent on MENA / Abu Dhabi / Hub71.** Nothing here supports or undermines the complementary-market slide. Do not cite this survey for Hub71.

**Interpretation:** Use this as **qualitative PH-young-adult signal**, not as proof of TAM or of SEA/MENA expansion.

## 08 — Competition

The survey never named Musixmatch, Genius, Spotify, Apple Music, Instagram, TikTok, or X. It still maps onto the four boxes:

| Deck box | What the data does |
|---|---|
| Lyric databases | People care about lines and lyrics (Q3, Q7, share formats). Silent on whether they *look up* lyrics on Genius/Musixmatch. |
| Streaming | Strong pull toward full songs, new music, recs, higher-quality listening. This is the gravity well. |
| General social | Public story/status sharing is already the default (89% sometimes/often). |
| Margo as dedicated home | Interest in “a concept like this” is high (50/56 very or somewhat interesting). That is not the same as leaving Instagram or Spotify. |

Open-ended complaints are mostly about **algorithms promoting only viral songs** and hiding smaller artists. That is a strike at streaming/social recs, which Margo can use in copy. It is also what every new music app claims.

## 09–11 — Fundraising, 12 months, the $10k ask

**Does this change the $10k plan?** No. It **reinforces** “prove they come back” and **weakens** any temptation to treat stated interest as traction.

| Deck move | Survey implication |
|---|---|
| $6,500 marketing in the Philippines (TikTok, Facebook, creators) | Friends-on-the-platform is a huge *likelihood* lever (86%). Spend that makes the first circle visible is aligned. This is still not evidence those ads will convert. |
| $2,000 keep the product running | No data. |
| $500 artists / first songs | Artist presence raises stated likelihood more than “following artists” was picked as a feature. A small artist seed is consistent. Open-ended demand for independents is real. 50 artists / 100+ songs is a supply bet this listener survey cannot size. |
| Delaware $500 / buffer $500 | No data. |
| 1,000 active users as validation; 5,000 stretch | **Unsupported.** 56 people saying they would use “several times a day” if they enjoyed it is classic intent inflation. Do not put survey intent next to the 1,000-user test as if they are the same kind of number. |
| Premium later | $1–$2 is the number people type when asked. Year-one plan should stay free. If premium is designed, “no ads + listening quality + personalization” matches WTP better than “advanced social.” |
| Ads later | More acceptable here than the deck’s caution suggests. Still correct to wait until there is a feed worth sitting in. |
| Bootstrapped / no institutions | Not a survey question. |

**Network-effect reading for PH-first, artist-discovery-driven growth**

- **Acquisition:** friends and liked artists both move stated likelihood a lot (86% each). Artist presence has a slightly stronger “much more likely” tail (52% vs 43%).
- **Retention (stated):** friends and artists are *not* the top comeback reasons. Mood, meaningful moments, recommendations, and new music are.
- **So what:** a PH launch that only seeds 50 artists and hopes the flywheel spins will miss what this group says they return for, unless those artists and those posts *are* the mood/moments layer. A launch that only buys user ads and has a thin catalog will miss the artist-presence lift. The deck already tries to do both (artists months 1–4, marketing all year). The survey says **do not drop either**, and **do not confuse “I would join if friends/artists were there” with “I will open this daily to Lyric Back.”**

## Direct answers to the research questions

**Does the data support that people already communicate through songs?**  
Yes. This is the cleanest result. Sending, receiving, and public sharing as expression are majority, high-frequency behaviors in this sample.

**Does it support Lyric Back / lyric-as-reply as a real behavior, not just a hypothetical?**  
It supports lyric-as-*message* as a real behavior. It does **not** show lyric-as-*reply in a product feed* as a demanded feature. Closest proxies (responding through music, conversations, notifications) are mid-to-low. Do not tell an investor this survey proved Lyric Back. Tell them it proved the 1:1 habit Lyric Back is modeled on.

**Does it support lyric cards / shareable visual moments?**  
Yes as a **utility for a moment they already want to take off-platform** (84% useful, 93% would share out). Weaker as a reason to adopt the app. Screenshoting is not how most of them share today; parts, lyric text, and video are.

**Friends/artists and PH-first artist-discovery growth?**  
Friends and artists are on-ramps. They are not, in this form, the daily engine. PH-first is consistent with who answered. Artist discovery through *other people’s posts* is wanted by a third of the sample; discovery by emotion/situation by about half; generic “new music” by two thirds.

**Pricing vs “premium later,” the 12-month plan, and the $10k ask?**  
$1–$2/month is the realistic *stated* band, with a large “not sure / would not pay / less than $1” mass under it. That **does not** change the $10k allocation toward marketing and keeping the product up. It **does** warn against pitching year-one revenue. It **does** suggest that if premium is designed after proof, it should look more like ad-free listening than like a lyric-power-user SKU. It **does not** replace the 1,000-user behavioral test.

## What pushes back on the current direction

1. **Discovery-and-listen gravity.** The concept they want is heavy on new music, full songs, mood, and personalized recs. That is incumbent ground. Margo’s live wedge is the lyric unit. If the product chases this survey’s feature list, it becomes a worse Spotify.
2. **Lyric Back is under-requested relative to how central it is in the architecture.** Keep it. Measure it. Do not claim survey proof.
3. **Retention story mismatch.** Deck: come back because the interaction lives here. Sample: come back for mood and recommendations. Year one must produce *meaningful music moments* that feel like recs, not only threads.
4. **Switching cost unasked.** They already share on stories. High interest can sit on top of “I’ll keep doing this on Instagram.”
5. **Artist following is weaker than artist presence.** Seeding 50 Filipino independents helps “the room has music I like.” It may not create a follow graph.
6. **Intent inflation.** 24/56 said they would use such a platform several times a day. Believe the habit questions (Q4–Q12) more than the platform questions (Q13–Q16).
7. **Instrument flaws.** Broken select-all on Q6, extra picks on Q14, no location, concept copy missing from the export, likely network sample.

## Implications for the deck and the ask

Do **not** fold raw percentages into the investor deck as traction. This is not usage.

If anything is later added to the deck, keep it qualitative and scoped:

- Fair: “In a small August 2026 survey of people in our first-market network, almost everyone already sent or posted a song to say something. Lyrics and specific lines carried the meaning as often as the whole track.”
- Fair: “They said they would share a designed card outside the app. They already share parts of songs and lyric text, mostly on stories and chats.”
- Fair: “Friends and artists on the platform would make them more likely to try it. That supports PH marketing plus a first artist community, not a paid launch.”
- Not fair: “89% would use Margo daily.” “57% will pay.” “Lyric Back is the #1 requested feature.” “MENA is validated.”

The $10,000 ask stays the right shape: **buy a year of proof in the Philippines, with enough music on the product that the habit has somewhere to land.** The survey makes that ask more credible on the *problem* side and more demanding on the *metric* side. The only number that should still close the deck is the one you will not have until month 12: whether people come back.
